Dart Turret Wiring

Power and signal wiring for the turret controller, with the main fuse, power switch and hardware arm switch. Matches firmware/turret/src/board.rs and docs/wiring.md. Status: proposed, not yet wired.

Highlight

Power

  • Unplug the XT60 to turn the turret off. At 4S the spark from the ESC capacitors is small; plug in quickly and firmly, without wiggling.
  • The ESC capacitor inrush on plug-in can trip the BMS short-circuit protection. Plug in a few times on the bench; if the turret ever stays dark, use a BMS that recovers by itself or an anti-spark XT60 with a pre-charge resistor.
  • Put the fuse as close to battery + as you can, on the pack side of the XT60, so it protects every wire after it.
  • The + and − buses are a quadcopter power distribution board (PDB). Every + pad on it is the same copper, and so is every − pad, so each lead in the table below can go on any pad of the right polarity.
  • Without a PDB, use two Wago 221-415 lever connectors per rail, joined by a short 14 AWG jumper: each rail has six leads and a 221-415 takes five. Solder the 26 AWG divider wires to a buck input lead, as they are below the Wago's minimum. Do not use screw terminals on bare stranded wire; they loosen under vibration.
  • Solder the 39 kΩ directly to the PDB VCC pad and put the 8.2 kΩ and 100 nF next to the ESP. The 26 AWG wire then carries only the divided voltage, so a chafed wire shorted to ground draws under 0.5 mA instead of burning before the 25 A fuse blows.
  • Two stalled SC15s draw 3.4 A, so the 7.5 V buck must be rated 5 A. The original 8 V MP1584 (3 A peak) is too small.
  • The MG996R pusher stalls at about 2.5 A and draws spikes that size each time it starts to move. Solder a 1000 µF, ≥16 V low-ESR electrolytic across its leads at the servo end, after pole A: + to the red lead, − to the brown lead. A reversed electrolytic heats up and can burst.
  • With pole A open, the capacitor runs the servo for only a few milliseconds, far too short to push a dart, so the arm switch still cuts the pusher.
  • If an ESC has a BEC, leave its red servo-lead wire unconnected. Only signal and ground go to the ESP.
  • Unplug the XT60 before plugging in USB, unless you have checked that the Freenove board isolates VBUS from its 5V pin.

Battery pack

The printed holder takes the four cells as two stacks of two, lying front to back. Contact plates in the covers make the series links, so no wire runs between cells. The cells come out for charging in a standalone 18650 charger that charges each cell to 4.2 V, so the pack needs no balance lead.

  • The back bridge link carries the full pack current: 14 AWG, or nickel strip at least 8 mm wide and 0.3 mm thick. Lay it in the long channel of the back cover.
  • Solder the plate tabs before the plates go into the covers, with the cells out.
  • Let the cover slots locate the plates, then fix them with 2-part epoxy on the edges and tabs only, never on the contact face or the spring coils. Avoid hot glue (softens near 60 °C) and superglue (cracks with cell swaps).
  • Strain-relieve every wire on the cover a few cm from its solder joint. Pulled wires are what tear plates loose.
  • Mark + and − next to every pocket. A reversed cell makes the pack read about 8 V; the firmware then refuses to arm.
  • Use unprotected high-drain cells. Protected cells are longer and limit the current far below the spin-up peak.
  • The 8 contacts are the weak point: at a 35 A spin-up peak, 10 mΩ each sags the pack by almost 3 V. Use thick plates, print the covers in PETG or ASA, and measure the drop across a contact during a spin-up; over 0.1 V needs attention.
  • Charge all four cells together and put them back as a set.

Firmware battery guard

The firmware reads the pack every 5 s while the flywheels are idle, so spin-up sag does not count. It only sees the whole pack; the BMS protects each cell.

Signals

  • If ping 0 gets no answer, swap the TTLinker RXD and TXD wires. These boards do not agree on whose side the labels name.
  • The pusher signal goes through the shifter like in the original: some digital servos on 6 V read 3.3 V poorly and jitter. The ESCs stay on 3.3 V signals, which BLHeli reads fine.
  • BSS138 level shifters are slow. If the servo bus shows errors at 1 Mbaud, use a 74AHCT125 buffer or lower the servo baud rate.
  • The light gate reads low while the beam is lit and high while a dart blocks it. The original wired the phototransistor from 5 V, which can push more than 3.3 V into the pin.

Use stranded silicone wire throughout so it survives the pan and tilt movement, and strain-relieve it where it crosses the gimbal axes.

First power-on

  1. With nothing on the buck outputs, set them to 7.5 V, 6.0 V and 5.0 V with a multimeter.
  2. Check that the + bus and − bus are not shorted, with the battery disconnected.
  3. With the cells in, measure the pack leads: 12.8–16.8 V. About 8 V means a reversed cell.
  4. First power-on: arm switch off, no darts, flywheel motors unplugged from the ESCs.
  5. Run B on the console and compare with a multimeter across the pack.
  6. Run ping 0 and ping 1. No reply: swap TTLinker RXD/TXD.
  7. Run status and flip the arm switch; it should show on and off.
  8. Calibrate both ESCs to 1000–2000 µs before the first spin-up.
